# Money Mile — Player Manual

**Players:** 3–4 (4 recommended for a first game) · **Age:** 16+ ·
**Play time:** 90–150 minutes

---

## 1. What this game is

Money Mile is a competitive strategy game about building and legitimising a
nightlife business empire. You acquire Properties, appoint Mamasans, recruit
Workers, install Upgrades, generate revenue, and compete for the city's most
valuable districts.

There are two economies.

**Clean operations** are expensive to establish but provide reliable income,
stronger assets and safer long-term growth.

**Underground operations** are cheaper and generate more revenue quickly, but
produce Dirty Cash, Heat, instability and permanent Notoriety.

**The player who generates the most money will not necessarily win.** Victory
depends on converting economic growth into an empire that can survive Police
pressure, attacks from opponents, and the Final Raid.

Every turn asks the same question:

> How much risk are you willing to carry to grow faster than everyone else?

---

## 2. How to win

### Clean Wealth Victory

Declare at the end of your turn when you have:

| | 4 players | 3 players |
|---|---|---|
| Clean Net Worth | $5.5m | $6.5m |
| Clean Cash | $1.5m | $1.5m |
| Lead over **every** opponent | $500k | $500k |

Every opponent then gets one final turn. If you still meet the conditions when
your next turn begins, you win.

### Empire Victory

Declare when you:

- own at least **10 of the 24 Properties**
- control at least **two districts**
- operate at least **50%** of your total Worker Capacity
- have a **Mamasan in every operating Property**
- have no more than **two closed Properties**

Every opponent gets one final turn, same as above.

### Final Raid Victory

The Final Raid card is hidden in the last cards of the deck. When it appears,
every player completes one final Night, a city-wide Raid is resolved, and the
**highest surviving Clean Net Worth** wins.

---

## 3. Clean Net Worth

Not the same as total cash. At scoring:

| Asset | Counts as |
|---|---|
| Clean Cash | 100% |
| Licensed Properties | 75% of purchase value |
| Legal Upgrades | 50% of purchase value |
| Unlicensed Properties | 25%, if they survive |
| **Dirty Cash** | **zero** |
| **Underworld Upgrades** | **zero** |
| Closed or seized assets | zero |

Dirty Cash helps you build an empire. It cannot directly win the game.

---

## 4. Setup

Each player starts with $700k Clean, $200k Dirty, one Starter Property, one
Mamasan, one Vetted Worker, one Unregistered Worker, three cards, Heat 1 and
Notoriety 0. Play proceeds clockwise from a random first player.

---

## 5. Your dashboard

- **Clean Cash** — safe money, used for legal investment and victory.
- **Dirty Cash** — risky money from underground operations.
- **Heat (0–10)** — current Police attention. Cold 0–2, Warm 3–5, Hot 6–8,
  Burning 9–10. Moves both ways.
- **Notoriety (0–5)** — permanent law-enforcement reputation. **Heat may never
  fall below your Notoriety.**
- **Clean Net Worth** — your victory-relevant value.
- **Capacity Utilisation** — active Workers ÷ total Worker Capacity.

---

## 6. Properties

24 Properties across six districts, each with a price, Worker Capacity, Safety,
Base Heat, Revenue Multiplier, Upgrade Slots, upkeep and licence status.

They are **not** a simple weakest-to-strongest ladder. A cheap underground venue
may be the best early buy. A Cash Business may earn little directly but make
laundering possible. A fortified Property may earn less but become essential
late.

---

## 7. Mamasans

**Every operating Property requires exactly one Mamasan.** A Mamasan runs only
one Property at a time.

A Property without one:

- produces **no revenue**
- cannot recruit Workers
- cannot use its special abilities
- still pays half its upkeep

Buying a Property is only half the purchase.

---

## 8. Workers

All Workers are consenting adults. "Unregistered" is a regulatory status only.

**Vetted Workers** generate Clean Cash, produce less immediate revenue, create
little Heat, have higher Stability, and are harder to poach.

**Unregistered Workers** generate Dirty Cash, are cheaper, earn more immediately,
generate Heat, may increase Notoriety, and are more vulnerable to Police and
disruption.

A Worker may be assigned to only one Property, and a Property may never exceed
its Worker Capacity.

### Conditions

**Hot** — earns extra. **Protected** — cannot be poached or targeted.
**Unavailable** — generates nothing. **Disrupted** — generates reduced revenue.

### Vetting

One Action and $150k Clean converts an Unregistered Worker to Vetted: they
generate Clean Cash, base revenue drops $25k (minimum $100k), Worker Heat becomes
zero, and Stability rises by one. **Existing Notoriety is not removed.**

---

## 9. Upgrades

Four categories, limited by each Property's Upgrade Slots.

- **Revenue** — VIP Room, Premium Bar, Event Space, Marketing Office
- **Security** — Cameras, Reinforced Doors, Safe Room, Private Security
- **Operations** — Extra Rooms, Staff Transport, Management Office, Recruitment Desk
- **Underworld** — Hidden Room, Cash Front, Offshore Books, Police Contact

Underworld Upgrades commonly generate Heat, Notoriety or Final Raid
vulnerability, and score **zero**.

---

## 10. The turn — one Night

> **Draw → Scheme → Operate → Resolve → Police → Close**

1. **Draw** one card. Environment cards resolve immediately for everyone.
2. **Scheme** — play one eligible card, or hold it.
3. **Operate** — take two Actions.
4. **Resolve** — calculate Potential Revenue, spin the Night Wheel, collect, and
   resolve any Trouble or Raid.
5. **Police** — one unit moves, proximity raises Heat, enforcement resolves.
6. **Close** — pay upkeep and Empire Overhead, update Heat and Notoriety, expire
   effects, check victory, pass play.

### Actions

Buy Property · Recruit Worker · Move Worker · Hire Mamasan · Move Mamasan · Buy
Upgrade · Licence Property · Vet Worker · Launder Cash · Reduce Heat · Repair
Upgrade · Reopen Property

You may take the same Action twice.

---

## 11. The Night Wheel

| Outcome | Effect | Baseline |
|---|---|---:|
| Big Night | 150% of Potential Revenue | 20% |
| Normal Night | 100% | 50% |
| Quiet Night | 50% | 20% |
| Trouble | 50% plus an adverse effect | 8% |
| Raid | Enforcement before any revenue | 2% |

Heat, Police, Safety, Cards, Mamasans and Upgrades all move these numbers. **You
can always see exactly why** — click any outcome in the game to expand its
contributions.

---

## 12. Heat, Property Heat and Notoriety

**Heat** rises from Unlicensed Properties, Unregistered Workers, Police
proximity, aggressive cards, Underworld Upgrades and failed laundering. It falls
through the Reduce Heat Action, licensing, Security and legal Upgrades, certain
Mamasans, Protection cards, and closing Properties.

**Property Heat** is local, and drives Raid targeting, closure risk and licence
challenges. A player can run moderate overall Heat while one venue is dangerous —
risk can be isolated rather than spread evenly.

**Notoriety** is gained by finishing two consecutive turns at Heat 8+, suffering
a Major Raid, running four or more Unregistered Workers for three consecutive
rounds, playing three Dirty Tricks in one round, or from specific cards and
Upgrades. It is very hard to remove, and the more you have, the harder it becomes
to return to a genuinely low-risk strategy.

---

## 13. Laundering

Dirty Cash does not automatically become Clean. Laundering costs one Action.

| Tier | Dirty | Clean | Retention |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $300k | $200k | 66.7% |
| 2 | $300k | $150k | 50.0% |
| 3 | $300k | $100k | 33.3% |
| 4 | $300k | $50k | 16.7% |

Without specialised infrastructure only Tier 1 is available, once per turn. Cash
Businesses and Underworld Upgrades unlock better tiers. **Large-scale laundering
is deliberately inefficient.**

---

## 14. Police

**Local Patrol** moves one hex and raises Property Heat nearby. **Vice Squad**
moves up to two and is especially dangerous to Unregistered Workers. **Financial
Crimes Unit** moves one, has a wider radius, and targets Dirty Cash.

| Distance | Pressure |
|---|---:|
| Same hex | +3 |
| Adjacent | +2 |
| Two hexes | +1 (Financial Crimes only) |

---

## 15. Raids

Severity compares **exposure** (Heat, Notoriety, Property Heat, Police proximity,
licence status, Unregistered Workers, Underworld Upgrades) against **protection**
(Safety, Security Upgrades, Mamasan abilities, Protection cards, voluntary
closure).

Consequences scale with severity: Dirty Cash seized, Workers removed, Properties
closed, Upgrades disabled, Heat and Notoriety added.

Raids should hurt. They should not normally remove a player from meaningful
participation.

---

## 16. Upkeep and expansion

Each Property has recurring upkeep, and every player pays Empire Overhead:

| Properties | Overhead |
|---:|---:|
| 1–4 | $0 |
| 5 | $50k |
| 6 | $100k |
| 7 | $200k |
| 8 | $300k |
| 9 | $450k |
| 10+ | +$200k each |

This is why expansion is not automatically correct. Large empires need real
utilisation and operating efficiency.

**Unpaid upkeep:** for every unpaid $50k, choose one — gain 1 Heat, disable an
Upgrade, close a Property until next round, or take $100k Dirty emergency finance
and gain 1 Notoriety.

---

## 17. Districts

Control a district by owning **three of its four** Properties.

| District | Control bonus |
|---|---|
| Laneways | First Unregistered Worker each round costs $50k less |
| Waterfront | First Big Night each round earns an extra $100k Clean |
| Bazaar | One Property gains +1 Capacity |
| Transit | First Worker movement each turn is free |
| Uptown | Licensed Properties gain +1 Safety |
| Old Quarter | First Heat-reduction Action removes one extra Heat |

Owning all four grants a bonus card draw. Concentration is efficient — and puts
several Properties within reach of one Police unit.

---

## 18. Cards

72 cards: 18 Opportunity, 18 Dirty Trick, 18 Protection, 18 Environment.
Opportunity grows your engine, Dirty Trick disrupts rivals, Protection cancels
and defends, Environment changes conditions for everyone. Reaction cards can be
played outside your own turn.

---

## 19. Strategies

**The Operator** — Licensed Properties, Vetted Workers, stable Clean Cash. Strong
late, slow early.

**The Hustler** — Unlicensed and Unregistered, fast growth. Powerful early,
vulnerable to Heat, laundering limits and Raids.

**The Shark** — cards and Police movement to punish exposed rivals. Strong against
reckless empires, weaker at building its own.

**The Fortress** — Safety and Security. Hard to attack, can fall behind
economically.

**The Baron** — Networks and Districts. Efficient territory, high upkeep and
concentrated Police risk.

**The Hybrid** — changes approach as the game develops. Most successful players
will need to adapt.

---

## 20. Final advice

A large pile of Dirty Cash may represent power. It may also represent unfinished
work.

Watch the Police, the deck, your opponents' Clean Net Worth, your laundering
capacity, your upkeep, and the location of your most exposed Property.

The strongest player is not the one who avoids risk. It is the one who knows
which risks remain worth taking.
